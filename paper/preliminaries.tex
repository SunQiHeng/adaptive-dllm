\section{Preliminaries: Diffusion Language Models}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs), also referred to as diffusion-based large language models (DLLMs), represent a paradigm shift from conventional autoregressive language modeling approaches. This section formalizes the basic notation for DLMs and their attention mechanisms.

\subsection{Attention Mechanism}

We consider a transformer-based DLLM with $N$ layers and $H$ attention heads per layer. Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$ denote the input hidden states of one layer, where $L$ is the sequence length and $d$ is the model dimension. Each attention head has dimension $d_h = d / H$. Unlike autoregressive LLMs, DLLMs use \emph{bidirectional} attention during denoising, so each token can attend to all other tokens in the sequence rather than only to its left context.

For head $h \in \{1,\dots,H\}$, the query, key, and value matrices are
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Q}_h = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_h^Q, \qquad
    \mathbf{K}_h = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_h^K, \qquad
    \mathbf{V}_h = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_h^V,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_h^Q, \mathbf{W}_h^K, \mathbf{W}_h^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_h}$ are the projection matrices for head $h$, and $\mathbf{Q}_h,\mathbf{K}_h,\mathbf{V}_h \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_h}$. The attention output of head $h$ is then
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{O}_h = \mathbf{A}_h \mathbf{V}_h
    = \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_h \mathbf{K}_h^\top}{\sqrt{d_h}}\right)\mathbf{V}_h,
    \label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A}_h \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$ denotes the attention-weight matrix for head $h$.

The multi-head attention output is obtained by concatenating the outputs of all heads and applying an output projection:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{MultiHead}(\mathbf{X}) =
    \operatorname{Concat}(\mathbf{O}_1,\dots,\mathbf{O}_H)\mathbf{W}^O,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}^O \in \mathbb{R}^{Hd_h \times d}$ is the output projection matrix. This bidirectional attention mechanism enables DLLMs to exploit global context at each denoising step, while different heads may specialize in different reconstruction behaviors. This head-level specialization makes attention-head attribution a natural tool for understanding and optimizing DLLMs.

\subsection{Core Framework}

Diffusion Large Language Models (DLLMs) define a model distribution $p_\theta(x_0)$ through two complementary stochastic processes: a \textit{forward process} that progressively corrupts data, and a \textit{reverse process} that iteratively denoises and recovers the original sequence.

Given a sequence $x_0 = (x_{0,1}, \dots, x_{0,L}) \in \mathcal{V}^L$, where $\mathcal{V}$ is the vocabulary and $L$ is the sequence length, the forward process gradually introduces noise by replacing tokens with $\texttt{[MASK]}$ according to a masking schedule. For $t \in [0,1]$, the corruption distribution is:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    q_t(x_t | x_0) &= \prod_{i=1}^L q_t(x_{t,i} | x_{0,i}), \\
    \text{where} \quad q_t(x_{t,i} | x_{0,i}) &= 
    \begin{cases}
        1 - \alpha(t) & \text{if } x_{t,i} = x_{0,i}, \\
        \alpha(t)    & \text{if } x_{t,i} = \texttt{[MASK]}, \\
        0            & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
\end{split}
\label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha: [0,1] \to [0,1]$ is a monotonically increasing masking schedule with $\alpha(0) = 0$ and $\alpha(1) = 1$. For example, one may use $\alpha(t)=t$.

The reverse (denoising) model is parameterized by a neural network $f_\theta$ that produces token logits conditioned on the masked sequence $x_t$ and time $t$. The training objective minimizes the reconstruction loss at masked positions:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{\substack{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1] \\ x_0 \sim p_{\text{data}} \\ x_t \sim q_t(x_t | x_0)}} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^L \mathbf{1}\!\left[x_{t,i} = \texttt{[MASK]}\right] \log p_\theta(x_{0,i} \,|\, x_t, t) \right]
    \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    p_\theta(x_{0,i} \,|\, x_t, t)
    =
    \left[\mathrm{softmax}([f_\theta(x_t, t)]_i)\right]_{x_{0,i}}
\end{equation}
is the predicted probability of the clean token $x_{0,i}$ at position $i$.

Unlike autoregressive models that generate tokens sequentially, DLLMs generate text through an iterative denoising process over discrete timesteps. Let $1=t_T>\cdots>t_1>t_0=0$ be a discretization of $[0,1]$. Starting from $x_{t_T}$, typically a fully masked sequence, the model progressively reduces the masking level. At each step $k$, the model estimates $p_\theta(x_0 \,|\, x_{t_k}, t_k)$, and $x_{t_{k-1}}$ is obtained by updating a subset of masked positions according to the decoding schedule. This non-autoregressive generation enables parallel token prediction and global context awareness during synthesis.
