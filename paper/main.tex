\begin{abstract}
Diffusion large language models (DLLMs) have attracted increasing attention for their ability to generate tokens in parallel, offering the potential to reduce the computational cost of both training and inference. Beyond efficiency, understanding how different attention heads contribute to DLLMs is also important for improving interpretability and enabling practical optimization techniques such as head pruning and adaptive sparse inference. However, existing head attribution methods are largely designed for autoregressive large language models and are not well suited to DLLMs, whose generation process involves iterative denoising across multiple refinement steps. Directly applying such methods to DLLMs is often computationally expensive and fails to fully account for their step-wise generation dynamics. To address this challenge, we propose a step-wise attribution method tailored to DLLMs, which efficiently estimates attention head importance by leveraging their iterative generation process. Extensive experiments show that our method produces effective head importance estimates while substantially reducing attribution cost.
\end{abstract}
